# Copy

For every word a person reads: headlines, buttons, labels, errors, empty states, emails, a README. Generated copy fails the same way each time: it sounds like a product page and says nothing that is true of this product only.

## Before writing

Know three things. If you do not, read the product or ask.

- Who is reading, and what they already know.
- What the product does, as a verb and an object.
- One fact a competitor cannot claim.

## Four tests

- **Swap.** Put a competitor's name in the sentence. If it is still true, it says nothing. Rewrite it around the fact that makes it false for them.
- **Proof.** Every claim carries a number, a name or an example the reader can check, or it goes. "Fast" becomes "cold start in 40 ms". With no real number, describe what happens; never invent one.
- **Spoken.** Would one person say this sentence to another across a desk? "We empower teams to unlock their potential" fails.
- **Delete.** Remove the sentence. If nothing was lost, leave it out. Try it first on the opening sentence of every paragraph.

## Write

- Lead with what the reader gets or does, in concrete nouns and plain verbs: "Send invoices from your terminal", not "The modern invoicing platform".
- The headline says the thing. The subhead adds how or for whom; it does not restate the headline.
- A feature heading states what the user can now do, not a category ("See which invoices are overdue", not "Analytics").
- The reader's word for a thing, and the same word every time it appears.
- Sentence case. Numerals. Present tense. "You" for the reader; "we" only when the company acts.
- One idea per sentence. Length varies because the content does.

## Microcopy

- **Button**: verb and object ("Create invoice", not "Submit"). A confirming button repeats the verb ("Delete 3 invoices", not "Yes").
- **Confirmation**: state the consequence ("This deletes 3 invoices and cannot be undone"), not "Are you sure?".
- **Error**: what happened, what to do next, in the user's terms. No blame, no "Oops", no bare code.
- **Empty state**: what goes here and the one action that adds it.
- **Success**: what changed ("Invoice sent to anna@example.com"), not "Success!".
- **Link**: says where it goes. Never "click here"; never "Learn more" with nothing after it.
- Counts pluralize correctly. Old timestamps become absolute dates.

## Tells

Rewrite any of these on sight. Do not swap a listed word for its synonym: the sentence was empty, so replace it with a fact.

- **Words**: seamless, effortless, powerful, robust, intuitive, cutting-edge, revolutionary, game-changing, next-generation, world-class, blazing fast, all-in-one, elevate, unlock, unleash, empower, supercharge, streamline, transform, reimagine, leverage, harness, journey, solution, ecosystem.
- **Adverbs that promise ease**: simply, just, easily, seamlessly, effortlessly, in seconds.
- **Headline shapes**: "Not just X, it's Y". "X, reimagined". "Meet X". "The future of X". "X made simple". "Your X, your way". "Built for the modern Y". Verb-your-noun ("Supercharge your workflow").
- **Reflex triples**: "Fast, simple, secure." "No setup. No config. No hassle."
- **Openers**: "Tired of X?" "What if X?" "In today's fast-paced world". "Whether you're an X or a Y".
- **Closers**: a last line that sums up the page or asks "Ready to transform your X?".
- **Hedges**: "can help you", "designed to", "aims to".
- **Friendly filler in the app**: "Welcome back!", "Oops! Something went wrong", "You're all set!", "Hang tight".
- **Typography**: the em dash as the default joint, exclamation marks, emoji in headings and bullets, Title Case On Everything.
- **The CTA pair**: "Get started" beside "Learn more".
